# Changelog

All changes to Castle Defense, newest first. Each entry says what changed for the player (or the project), not how the code works.

## 2026-10-30
- **Combat hits harder.** Catapult shots now give the screen a little jolt, and a boss going down shakes it properly (turn **Screen shake** off in Settings if you prefer a steady view). Killing a boss, Black Knight or Troll freezes the action for a split second, gold coins fly from every kill into your gold counter, and each wave opens with a big banner – "Wave 7", "Final wave", or a red "Boss incoming" naming the boss.
- **The level list is now a world map.** Choose a level by its place on an illustrated map of the kingdom: a road winds from Greenmeadow through forests, marshes, ruins, sea cliffs, deserts and snowy mountains to The Sundered Realm at the edge of the world. Each stop shows your stars, the road lights up as you win, and locked lands wait behind a padlock. Click a stop to see its card – description, level twist, board size and any run in progress – then Play (or double-click it). On phones the map scrolls sideways.
- **New enemy: the Pirate.** Goblin buccaneers in tricorn hats, with a cutlass, an eye patch, a peg leg and a bottle of rum, now storm ashore in most of Saltcliff Cove's waves. A badly wounded pirate takes a swig and charges half again as fast, and one that reaches the gate makes off with 25 of your gold as well as a life. They have their own Bestiary entry.
- **The eight new levels each get a twist of their own**, shown in the level picker and explained by a banner the first time it happens. **Harvest Vale – Harvest fields:** every wheat field you leave unbuilt brings in 3 gold after each wave, so the best tower spots cost you a harvest. **Saltcliff Cove – Pirate landings:** every third enemy on foot rows ashore halfway along the road. **Sunscorch Dunes – Heat mirages:** shimmering copies march among the enemy; they vanish at the first hit and cost no lives, but they soak up your towers' shots. **Frostpeak Hold – Blizzards:** in a blizzard towers attack 35% slower, unless a brazier or an Alchemist keeps them warm. **Crystal Hollow – Singing crystals:** towers built right next to a crystal deal 25% more damage. **Wolfjaw Pass – Rockfalls:** boulders crash onto the road, crushing enemies and blocking the pass for a few seconds – but bosses smash straight through. **Stormwatch Heights – Lightning strikes:** lightning hits your towers and overcharges them to fire 50% faster for 8 seconds. **The Sundered Realm – Void rifts:** twin rifts open on the two roads, and an enemy that steps into one comes out of the other – closer to the castle or farther away.
- **Eight new levels (9–16)**, each in a land of its own, unlocked after The Dread Wastes. **Harvest Vale** – golden autumn farmland with orchards, haystacks and falling leaves. **Saltcliff Cove** – sea cliffs where pirate ships wait in the bay below, with a wreck on the rocks and a smugglers' camp (and the Pirate King leading the landing party). **Sunscorch Dunes** – desert sand, palms around an oasis and old sandstone pillars, attacked from two sides. **Frostpeak Hold** – a mountain fortress in the snow, with falling snow, snowy pines and peaks behind the castle. **Crystal Hollow** – a twilight glade full of glowing crystals, where two roads meet at the gate. **Wolfjaw Pass** – one long, narrow road winding between towering rocks, with little room to build. **Stormwatch Heights** – wind, rain and lightning over the old standing stones, with thunder rolling in. **The Sundered Realm** – the final level, where the world breaks apart into floating rocks and glowing cracks, and the last wave brings every boss at once. Each has its own 20 hand-made waves and gets a little harder than the one before. The achievements for winning every level now mean all sixteen.
- **Ashfall, Barrow Moor, the Scorched Badlands and the Dread Wastes have their own twist too** (shown in the level picker, with a banner the first time it strikes). **Ashfall – Crumbling ruins:** in wave 7 the ruined houses in the middle of town collapse and open a shortcut, and from then on every other enemy on foot takes it. **Barrow Moor – Restless graves:** open graves beside the road glow green while a wave marches in, then skeletons climb out onto the road – more of them in later waves. **Scorched Badlands – Lava vents:** the lava pools rumble and erupt during waves, burning nearby enemies and stunning towers built right next to them. **Dread Wastes – Falling darkness:** every so often darkness falls for ten seconds, and towers lose 40% of their range unless a lava pool or a Banner Tower's torches light them – the glowing circles around the pools show where the light reaches. The tower panel tells you whether a tower is lit or sits next to a vent.
- **Darkwood, Emberwood and Mirefen each have their own twist**, shown under the level in the level picker. **Darkwood – Forest fog:** mist hangs over the cells along the wall, and towers built there lose a quarter of their range (the range ring and the tower's panel show it). **Emberwood – Wildfire:** during waves, embers land on the board and the fire spreads to the cells around it. Nothing can be built on burning ground until it dies down – a fire lit late in a wave can still be burning in the break. **Mirefen – Sucking bog:** parts of the road are flooded bog, and enemies on foot wade through at 60% speed – a good place for a Catapult. Flyers don't care.
- **Achievements:** 20 to collect, from winning without losing a life or with just one life left, to banishing the same Dragon three times, holding 1,000 gold, defeating every boss, surviving 30 waves in Endless mode or beating every level on Hard. A little banner with a fanfare pops up when you unlock one, and the new Achievements page in the menu shows them all, with your progress on the ones that need counting. Wins, stars, Endless records and perks you already have count straight away.
- **The Royal Armoury:** a new page in the main menu where your stars buy permanent perks – stronger Archers, siege engines or magic towers, tougher Barracks soldiers, cheaper upgrades, extra starting gold, an extra life or two, a stronger Sir Roland and faster spells. Each perk has two or three ranks and the bonuses are kept small, so the game stays a challenge. Every star you have earned counts, on every difficulty – and since a win on a harder difficulty also earns its stars on the easier ones, harder wins pay more. Changed your mind? Reset the perks and get all your stars back.
- **Endless mode:** once you have won a level, the ∞ button next to it in the level picker (or "Endless mode" on the victory screen) plays it again with no final wave. After the usual 20 waves, new waves keep coming – bigger every time, with enemies from every corner of the kingdom from wave 26 on, and more and more bosses every fifth wave. Your best (waves survived) is saved for each level and difficulty, shown on the ∞ button and in the wave counter, and the defeat screen tells you when you set a new record. Endless runs are saved between waves like normal ones.
- **Difficulty: Easy, Normal and Hard.** Pick one at the top of the level picker before you start a level. **Easy** is the game as it was (20 lives). **Normal** gives you 15 lives and 10% less starting gold, and enemies have 30% more health. **Hard** is a real challenge: 10 lives, 20% less starting gold and enemies with 75% more health – a boss slipping through hurts. Each difficulty has its own level unlocks, stars and saved runs. Winning on a harder difficulty also counts on the easier ones, so beating a level on Hard gives you the same stars (and unlocks) on Normal and Easy. Star thresholds follow your lives: 90% left for three stars, half for two. Your progress so far counts as Easy. The difficulty shows next to the level name at the top of the screen.
- **Stars:** winning a level now earns 1–3 stars for the lives you have left – three for 18 or more, two for 10 or more, one for any win. They appear on the victory screen (with "New best!" when you beat your old score) and next to each level in the level picker, which keeps your best result. Levels won before this update show a tick until you win them again.
- **Repair the gate:** when you've lost lives, a hammer button next to your lives (or the R key) buys one back for gold. The first repair costs 50 gold and each one after gets dearer.
- **Three new spells** replace Sir Roland's Shield Bash, Lance Charge and Rallying Cry on the hero bar (keys 1–3): **Rain of Arrows** – pick it, click a spot, and four volleys of arrows hammer everything there, bats included; **Boiling Oil** – cauldrons pour scalding oil over the three road tiles at the castle gate, setting the road ablaze and slowing everything in it; **Reinforcements** – click the road and two soldiers drop in to hold it for 15 seconds. Each has its own cooldown, effects and sounds. Divine Smite stays on key 4.
- **Sir Roland's attacks are easy to read now:** every stroke is a big overhead chop with a bright slash and sparks on the enemy, a ring on the ground shows how far he reaches (gold while he's fighting), and a red ring marks the enemy he's fighting.
- **Bosses can't be blocked by the Barracks** any more – soldiers let them walk past. Only Sir Roland can stand in a boss's way.
- **New: your hero, Sir Roland.** A knight on horseback who rides wherever you click on the road and holds the line there – he blocks and fights anything on foot, levels up (up to level 5) as enemies fall around him, and if he falls he rides back from the gate after 15 seconds. He has four abilities: **Shield Bash** (stuns everything around him), **Lance Charge** (hurls enemies back down the road), **Rallying Cry** (nearby towers attack faster) and **Divine Smite**, a blast of holy light that can be used once a wave and heals him fully.
- **Waves are now hero time:** while a wave is on you can't build or sell towers – the tower bar turns into the hero's bar, with his health, level and ability buttons (keys 1–4). You can still upgrade towers mid-wave.

## 2026-10-29
- **Barracks upgrade paths:** at level 3 the Barracks becomes a **Knights' Hall** – three armoured knights in silver plate who are far tougher, hit harder and shrug off half of every blow, even a boss's – or a **Warband** of four axe fighters in horned helmets who are back on their feet twice as fast. Tip: hold enemies where your towers can reach them.
- **New tower: Barracks.** Build it next to the road and it sends three soldiers in kettle hats to hold the road at a rally point. They block goblins, knights and anything else on foot, fight them sword to shield, and when one falls a new soldier marches out a few seconds later. Select the Barracks and click the road nearby to move the rally point. Bats simply fly over them. It's on key 9.
- **More upgrade paths:** every tower now picks a path at level 3. The **Ballista** becomes a **Skewer** whose bolts punch through a whole line of enemies, or a **Giant Slayer** that does double damage to bosses, Black Knights and Trolls. The **Alchemist** brews **Acid** that strips enemies' armour so your archers and catapults hit harder, or a **Plague** that spreads to nearby enemies when a poisoned one dies. The **Banner Tower** beats **War Drums** that make nearby towers attack faster, or raises a **Blessed Standard** that lets nearby towers ignore enemy resistances. The **Treasury** becomes a **Royal Bank** that pays interest on the gold you save, or a **Bounty Office** that pays extra for enemies killed near it. Each path has its own look.
- **Upgrade paths:** the level-3 upgrade of four towers is now a choice. **Archers** become **Longbowmen** (much longer range) or a **Crossbow Nest** (bolts pierce armour and hit harder). The **Catapult** throws **Fire Pots** that leave burning ground, or a huge **Boulder** that crushes whatever it lands on. The **Wizard** becomes an **Archmage** who banishes a whole group at once, or a **Time Warden** who leaves fields of slowed time on the road. The **Storm Tower** becomes a **Tempest** whose lightning jumps between many more enemies, or a **Thunderlord** whose heavy strikes stun everything they hit. Each path changes how the tower looks, and the inspector shows two cards to pick from (or press B / N).
- With the stronger level-3 towers, the game is a bit easier than before.
- **Hand-crafted waves:** every level now has its own 20 waves instead of the same pattern everywhere, and each one has a name, shown in the next-wave panel: tight **wolf rushes**, slow **knight pushes**, **shield walls**, long **mixed swarms**, **bone hordes** on Barrow Moor, **imp swarms** in the Badlands, **pincer** attacks down both roads at once, and a calm **breather** after every boss. New enemies arrive one land at a time, so you meet them more gradually, and the final bosses now come after their escort with a moment to aim at them.
- **Every level is winnable:** the later levels were rebalanced so a solid defence can hold all 20 waves, while each land still costs a few more lives than the last. Barrow Moor and the Dread Wastes in particular are no longer near-impossible.
- The wave button now says "two dragons" when two are coming.
- **Three new bosses** join the Dragon, and every level now has its own line-up of bosses for waves 5, 10, 15 and 20.
- **New boss: Goblin King** – a fat goblin in a crown and ermine who calls two more goblins to his side every few seconds.
- **New boss: Siege Ram** – a slow wooden battering ram pushed by goblins. If it reaches the gate it smashes it for **10 lives**.
- **New boss: Necromancer** – raises the enemies that fall near it as **Skeletons** (a new, weak enemy) every few seconds, and half resists magic.
- **Boss health bar:** while a boss is on the board, its name, health and how many lives it would cost are shown at the top of the screen.
- **Meet the enemy:** the first time you ever run into a new kind of enemy, the game pauses for a moment and a card shows what it looks like and what makes it special. It carries on by itself after a few seconds, or press **Space** / click **Got it**.
- **Bestiary:** a new page in the main menu lists every enemy you've met so far – with a picture, a description, its health, speed, gold and resistances, and where you first met it. The ones you haven't met yet are dark silhouettes.
- **New enemy: Ghost** – a hooded, see-through wraith that haunts **Barrow Moor** (and now and then Darkwood and the Dread Wastes). Every few seconds it fades almost out of sight for a second: while it's faded, no tower can target it, arrows already flying at it pass straight through, and splash and poison can't touch it.
- **New enemy: Fire Imp** – a little red devil with a flaming head and a pitchfork, found on the lava levels (**Scorched Badlands** and **The Dread Wastes**). It's immune to fire and poison, so Alchemists ignore it – bring other towers.
- Levels can now each have their own special enemies – that is how the Ghosts and Fire Imps keep to their home turf.
- **New enemy: Goblin Sapper** (from wave 9). You can spot it by the lit bomb it holds over its head. When it passes a tower next to the road, it leaves the road, sprints at the tower and blows up, knocking the tower out for 4 seconds (stars circle the stunned tower and it can't shoot). Kill it before it gets there and its bomb just fizzles. A stunned Banner Tower doesn't boost anyone until it recovers.
- **New enemy: Troll** (from wave 12). A big, slow brute with a club and a basket on its back with two goblins in it. When the Troll falls, the goblins jump out and keep running – so kill Trolls early, not right at the gate.
- The next-wave preview now explains an enemy's special ability only on the wave it first appears (resistances are always shown, and hovering an icon shows everything), so the panel stays small.
- **New enemy: Bat swarms** (from wave 6). A flock of bats that ignores the road and flies straight from the breach to the castle gate, so they arrive much sooner than anything on foot. Only **Archers, the Ballista, the Wizard and the Storm Tower** can hit them – Catapult boulders and Alchemist flasks can't reach them. The tower card shows "🦇 Hits flyers" or "Ground only", and the next-wave preview warns when bats are coming.
- **New enemy: Shieldbearer** (from wave 8). A stocky goblin behind a big tower shield that blocks three quarters of the damage from arrows and Ballista bolts that hit it from the front. You'll hear a clang and see grey numbers when it blocks. Shoot it from the side or behind, or use catapults, magic, poison or lightning, which go around the shield.
- **New enemy: Goblin Shaman** (from wave 10). It leans on a skull staff, and every few seconds it sends out a green pulse that heals the enemies around it (green numbers show how much). Dragons only get a little of it. Take the Shaman down first!
- The next-wave preview lists what the new enemies do, along with the other enemies' resistances.
- **Damage types and armour:** every tower now deals physical, magic, fire & poison or lightning damage (shown on the tower card). **Black Knights** wear heavy armour and shrug off half of all physical damage from Archers and Catapults, but they now have less health, so magic, poison and lightning cut through them. The **Ballista** pierces armour, and the **Storm Tower**'s lightning ignores it. **Dragons** shrug off half of the Alchemist's fire & poison. Resisted hits show grey damage numbers and armour-piercing hits show pale gold. The next-wave preview lists what the coming enemies resist.
- **Call waves early for bonus gold:** after each wave you get a 25-second break. Start the next wave before the time is up and you earn bonus gold – the more time left, the bigger the bonus, and it grows in later waves. The wave button shows the bonus and the seconds left.
- **Auto-start waves:** a new option in Settings starts the next wave by itself when the break runs out, so the battle keeps flowing. It's off by default.
- **See what a tower will reach:** while you choose where to place a tower, the road tiles inside its range light up in gold (red if you can't afford it yet). Selecting a tower shows the same.
- **Coverage map:** press **C** (or the new grid button by the minimap) to colour the whole road by how many towers reach each tile. Dark tiles are gaps nobody covers; blue, green, yellow, orange and red mean 1 to 5+ towers. While it's on, the tower you're placing is counted too, so you can see the gaps it would fill.
- **Upgrade and sell hotkeys:** with a tower selected, press **U** to upgrade it or **S** to sell it.
- **Undo:** changed your mind? A tower you place between waves can be taken back for a full refund, upgrades included, until you start the next wave. The Sell button turns into **Undo**, and **Ctrl+Z** takes back the last tower you placed. The help popover lists all the new keys.
- **Targeting modes:** select a tower to choose who it shoots at: **First**, **Last**, **Strongest**, **Fastest** or **Closest**. Press **T** to cycle through them. The Ballista still goes for the strongest enemy and everything else for the one in front, until you change it. Your choices are kept when you upgrade and are saved with your run.
- **Pause:** press **P** or the new pause button next to the speed buttons to freeze the battle. A "Paused" sign shows until you carry on, the music turns down, and you can still build, upgrade and sell towers while you think.
- **Game speed hotkey:** press **F** to switch between ×1, ×2 and ×3. At higher speeds all the animations, effects and even the music keep pace with the action.
- **Your progress is saved:** the game now remembers your run between waves. If you refresh the page, close the tab or quit, choose **Continue** in the menu to pick up where you left off, at the start of the next wave. The level list shows which levels have a run in progress.
- **Restart level:** the menu has a new **Restart level** button (click it twice, so you can't lose a run by accident), and the victory and defeat screens now offer **Restart level** too.
- **Project housekeeping:** added a balance simulator that plays through a level with a given set of towers in a few seconds, so future changes to difficulty can be tested properly before they reach you.
- **Works offline:** the game no longer needs to fetch its 3D engine from the internet when it starts, so it loads faster and keeps working on a shaky connection or with no connection at all (when run locally).
- **Project housekeeping:** added a roadmap of planned features (new enemies, upgrade paths, pause and game speed, spells, stars and more) to guide future updates.

## 2026-10-26
- **New-look towers:** every tower has been rebuilt with far more detail. They all stand on a rough stone plinth and have sloped stone feet, loose stones in the walls, studded doors with stone arches, arrow slits, corbelled battlements and plank floors. Each has its own touches: ivy and a barrel of spare arrows on the Archers (with shields on the walls once upgraded); spoked wheels, a banded counterweight crate, a winch and a pile of stones on the Catapult; glowing rune rings, a balcony, a turret and a magic circle on the Wizard tower, whose wizard now has a long beard and a crooked hat; buttresses and a proper crossbow with curved arms and a winch on the Ballista; copper pipes, insulators and a coiled spire on the Storm Tower; a bubbling cauldron over a fire, a copper still and a shelf of glowing potions on the Alchemist; long swallow-tailed banners and pennants on the Banner Tower; and an iron-studded vault door, a ribbed golden dome, treasure chests and a coin sack on the Treasury. The little crewmen have rounded bodies, faces and pointed hoods or iron hats.
- **New-look enemies:** every enemy has been rebuilt with far more detail. Goblins have long ears, tusks, beady eyes, open leather jerkins, belts with pouches and bone necklaces, and come in two kinds: helmeted ones with a round shield and spiked club, and mohawked ones in fur with a rusty cleaver. Wolf riders sit on shaggy wolves with a mane, bushy tail, spiked collar, snarling jaws and a red saddle blanket, carrying a pennant spear. Black knights wear full plate with spiked shoulder guards, a horned helmet with a plume, a red cape and tabard, a kite shield and a greatsword. The dragon has horns, glowing eyes, fangs, belly plates, a spiked back and tail, and proper bat-like wings with fingers and torn edges.
- **Better-looking ground:** the roads are no longer neat square tiles. They're now cobbled tracks that blend into the grass, with worn dirt edges, scattered edge stones and rough, uneven borders. Grass and earth no longer show an obvious repeating pattern, and the ground darkens where it meets the walls.
- **Soft shading where things meet:** towers, walls, trees, houses and the castle now have soft shadows at their bases and in their nooks, so everything sits firmly on the ground instead of floating. You can turn this off under Settings → Ambient occlusion if the game runs slowly.
- **Sharper surfaces:** stone, wood, roofs, rock and earth have twice as much detail on computers, and their shine varies (rougher in cracks and gaps). Shadows are crisper too. Phones and tablets keep the lighter settings so the game stays smooth.
- **A real moat:** the water around the castle is now a sunken channel with stone banks, deep water that gets shallower at the edges, gently moving ripples and lily pads. Its colour suits each land – clear blue in the fields, murky green in the marsh, dark and gloomy in the Dread Wastes.
- **8 levels:** the castle now has to be defended through eight lands, each worse than the last – the peaceful fields, a dark forest full of fireflies, a burning forest with burnt-out villages, a misty marsh, a smouldering ruined town, a haunted graveyard moor, scorched badlands with rivers of lava and finally the Dread Wastes under a blood-red sky. Every level has its own road, its own breach in the wall and its own spot for the castle, and some are bigger or smaller than others.
- **Harder to build:** later levels have tiles you can't build on (stumps, bog pools, rubble, graves, lava pits), roads that are harder to cover, and the last two levels have two breaches, so the enemy comes from two sides at once.
- **Levels menu:** win a level to unlock the next. Pick any unlocked level from Levels in the main menu, or go straight to the next one from the victory screen. The current level is shown under the title.
- **Moodier colours:** the world has a darker, more natural look – earthier grass, a softer sky, deeper water and toned-down colours everywhere – while torches, magic and fireworks still glow.
- The new colours were a little too grey and dark, so they're now a bit brighter and livelier while staying natural.
- **Brighter shadows:** the whole game is lighter, and shadows are soft and see-through instead of almost black.
- Right-click anywhere to deselect the tower you have selected.
- **New look:** the whole interface has been redesigned. The battlefield now fills the screen, with the controls floating on top as sleek glass panels in gold and dark wood tones. On phones the controls sit neatly below the battlefield.
- **Tower stats:** the info panel now shows each defender's DPS, damage, fire rate and range, with bars so you can compare towers at a glance. Selected towers also show what the next upgrade gives you, in green.
- **Next wave preview:** see how many goblins, wolf riders, knights and dragons are coming before you start a wave, and how many are left during it.
- Hover over a tower in the dock to see its stats before you buy it. A new ? button lists all the controls, and the speed buttons (×1, ×2, ×3) can be picked directly.
- Redesigned victory and defeat screens.
- **Main menu:** the game now opens on a main menu with Play, Settings, Changelog and Quit. Open it any time with the ☰ button or Esc – the game pauses while it's open, and Play picks up where you left off.
- **Settings:** turn music, sound effects, glow, shadows and high resolution on or off. Your choices are remembered; turning off the graphics options helps on slower phones and laptops.
- **Changelog in the game:** read what's new straight from the menu.
- **Live online:** the game is now hosted on GitHub Pages at https://lykhan28.github.io/slotsforsvar/ so friends can play it.
- **Fits any window:** the 3D view now fills the browser window at any size, with no page scrolling. On phones held upright, the camera widens so the whole board stays visible.
- **New Wizard role:** the Wizard now banishes enemies back along the road instead of freezing them.
- **English:** all in-game text translated from Danish to English; the title is now "Castle Defense".
- Fixed a black screen on start caused by the scenery loading before the towers.
- Project housekeeping: removed old duplicate files, added notes for working on the project.
